A pile-on can travel.

A written-only bundle misses interviews, radio discussions, podcasts and social clips. The useful question is how often the story was renewed, what each appearance added, and what evidence connects the people distributing it.

Added to the bundles

Six web-video records, one radio programme, one podcast and two official YouTube uploads. Three embedded formats link to records already in the written inventory. The 179 written records retain their own counts.

GB News appears through its original Bulley interview page. ITV, Sky, BBC Sport, Radio 4, LBC and Spiked sources supply other formats and perspectives. These are evidence to question, not automatic pile-on findings.

One programme, two slots

BBC Radio 4’s original page lists the Bulley Media Show episode at 16:30 and 21:30 on 22 February 2023. The same 28-minute programme also covers war reporting. This proves two listed broadcast slots; it does not establish 28 minutes about Bulley, listening figures or harm.

Reach needs a date

ITV’s official upload showed 56,500 views when checked on 14 October 2026. That is a cumulative platform counter, not the number of people who saw it in February 2023, a unique audience, or a measure of its effect.

Record views, shares or airtime only when supported by a source, with observation dates and scope. A broadcaster’s total audience or an account’s followers cannot substitute for this item’s audience.

Was it weaponised?

A wealthy sponsor or state could be an investigative hypothesis. Test commissioning, traceable payments, ownership/control, paid promotion, account links and distribution instructions. State which links are documented and which remain unknown.

Repeated framing, synchronized timing, political alignment or a guest’s employer cannot establish financing or coordination by themselves. None of these case bundles presently establishes a rich sponsor or foreign state directing a pile-on.

How the game now presents it

The timeline has a separate “Beyond the headline” panel. Filter TV/web video, radio/podcasts or social uploads, then open the original source. Source cards label dates, duration limits, related written items and known repeats. Questions to outlet editors distinguish their own recovered media from other outlets’ material.
