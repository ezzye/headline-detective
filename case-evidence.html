Research checked 14 October 2026

Look beyond a single headline.

Research, commentary and formal findings help test how repeated coverage frames a person. A legitimate initial story does not settle whether its repetition, emphasis or omissions are fair.

Play the jury →

Use the sources together. Examine the dated publication record, the public-interest developments, the language and images, and what later inquiries actually found. Structural bias can be investigated without an editor admitting intent. Claims of deliberate concealment require further evidence.

Sterling · wealth and scrutiny

Elliot Ross, Al Jazeera opinion · 12 December 2018 ↗

Ross interprets recurring attacks on Sterling’s ordinary life and prosperity as racialised scrutiny.

Authored commentary supplies a hypothesis and source leads. Its account of earlier headlines needs original-page verification; it is not a measured census or Al Jazeera’s institutional position.

Football · broadcast framing

PFA / RunRepeat study · 2020 ↗

Across 80 matches, more than 2,000 comments about 643 players showed differing descriptions by skin-tone group: intelligence and work ethic versus pace and power.

Live commentary across four European leagues and UK/US/Canadian media, not a UK press pile-on study. Groups came from Football Manager skin-tone labels, not self-identified ethnicity.

Football · X/Twitter concentration

PFA Charity / Signify · 21 October 2020 ↗

The selected 44-player study analysed 825,515 tweets during six weeks of Project Restart; half the recorded abuse targeted three players who had spoken against racism.

This is a selected 2020 online-abuse study. It does not establish that particular newspaper stories caused abuse, or reconstruct the 2018 Sterling or 2023 Greenwood windows.

A comparable framing study · Eniola Aluko

Velija & Silvani · peer-reviewed article, 2020 ↗

The authors analysed 80 print articles in three British newspapers from 6 August to 19 October 2017. They found racial and gender framing questioned Aluko’s belonging and legitimacy.

This is research on a Black woman reporting harassment, not evidence of the truth of allegations in another case. It provides a useful method for studying narrative framing over time.

Arday · representation context

Jason Arday · The black professoriate, 2022 ↗

Arday’s refereed paper synthesises evidence on barriers to Black academics entering the UK professoriate.

It is authored academic context, not research about the later coverage, and does not resolve the allegations. Read alongside the independent inquiry’s scope and eventual findings.

Flack · measured publication scale

Guardian original content analysis · 21 February 2020 ↗

Six-month print inventory: 387 national-newspaper stories mentioning Flack, including 99 in the Sun.

These are mentions, not 99 negative stories. The period and selection rule differ from the game’s dated sample. This is not a causal finding about her death.

Christopher Jefferies: wrongful suspicion and prejudicial press coverage · formal record

Leveson examines the concentrated character attacks · 2012-11-29 ↗

Leveson identifies the damaging three-day character coverage and records settlements by eight titles over 40 articles. Mirror editor Richard Wallace claimed off-record police briefings; Chief Constable Colin Port disputed this. The inquiry could not resolve that conflict.

Leveson is direct evidence about concentrated character attacks, not proof about a later case. The eight selected examples and 40 settled articles remain separate sets.

Meghan Markle: private-letter publication and the 2019 media wave · formal record

The appeal tests proportionate publication · 2021-12-02 ↗

The appeal upheld the privacy outcome concerning five challenged articles. The court concluded that publication was not a proportionate response to the People article.

The appeal concerns the proportionality of publishing the private letter in five articles. It is a specific privacy finding, not a judicial ruling on racism or every article about Markle.

Nicola Bulley: missing-person reporting, speculation and disclosure · formal record

An independent review examines the disclosure · 2023-11-21 ↗

The independent review concluded that releasing personal information on 15 February was avoidable and unnecessary, and examined police communications under intense news and social-media attention.

The review addresses policing and disclosure under pressure. Its findings are not an Ofcom ruling against every broadcaster, or a complete count of social posts.

Wider representation

Cardiff REACH media monitoring report (2011) ↗

Crime accounted for 66.9% of sampled items featuring Black young men and boys, against 41.9% for all young men and boys.

The comparator includes Black youth; the sample is from 2008–09 and includes victims. It does not mean 67% portrayed Black people as criminals.

Wider representation

Black British Voices Project (2023) ↗

Three in four respondents believed Black businesses were treated unfairly by investors and financial institutions. Media stereotyping and football double standards were also concerns in the report.

A survey and interviews document reported experience and perceptions. Business-investment findings cannot be relabelled as a measured media-coverage disparity.

Flack · reporting after her death

Jarvis and colleagues · PRINTQUAL-Web (2025) ↗

The study used 342 online articles about Flack’s death to develop a reporting-quality tool. It considers features such as comments, sharing and links, alongside responsible reporting standards.

This examines coverage after her death through 2021, not the earlier pile-on or its causes. It does not measure article prominence or audience reach; binary ratings may miss nuance. The abstract and dataset section differ on the end date, so no exact final day is asserted here.

Diallo: distinguish the actual sanction from the narrative

The FIGC’s original notice ↗, published 5 February 2021, records an agreed sports-disciplinary sanction under Article 126, with €48,000 fines for Diallo and Hamed Traore. It is a football-governance record, not a criminal-court conviction or proof that either player trafficked anyone.

Investigate how outlets described age, responsibility and the underlying documentation case. We have not yet assembled a dated multi-outlet Diallo inventory. The Mail links for the related house and breakfast stories were not retrievable in the web check; the accessible Sun originals provide the verified comparison above.

How this strengthens the game

Ask the outlet what each return added, why personal wealth or appearance became relevant, how replies and changed legal status were presented, and what it would have done in a comparable case. Research is contextual evidence; these references do not add articles to the game’s recovered counts.

For Greenwood, proceedings were discontinued before trial: there was no guilty or not-guilty verdict. For Arday, an inquiry’s announcement is distinct from its findings. A named columnist’s view does not establish an owner’s instruction.
