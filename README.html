Headline Detective

The new Jason Arday round asks how four outlets’ coverage changed over 22 days, and what public value or pressure that pace may represent.

That is 90 listed pieces selected from NewsCord’s fixed inventory of 229 items across 15 outlets. The unit is a distinct substantive written piece. Passing mentions, broadcasts and video-only items are excluded; indexing coverage is qualified, so totals may be lower bounds.

NewsCord inventory and method

What you judge

Compare daily counts, active days, peaks, and the timing of documented developments. Decide whether the campaign’s amount and pace were proportionate. You can suggest a count you consider proportionate, but neither verdict nor count earns points automatically. Five evidence checks are worth two points each; 8/10 wins.

Volume alone does not establish motive, intent, discrimination, or harm. A resignation does not automatically end public interest; the later inquiry is a documented development that may affect your judgment.

Examples and audit

The round shows 11 selected headline examples. They illustrate the record and are not the counting population. BBC and Telegraph examples are six-word inventory excerpts because original publisher pages were unavailable to this build. Other examples have page-check notes; retrieved versions may reflect later edits.

campaign-records.csv is the daily-count audit: dates and URLs only. It records the underlying dated rows without adding headline text.

Run locally

  1. Install Node.js 22 or later.
  2. In a terminal opened in this folder, run:
npm start

Open http://127.0.0.1:9027/. No packages need installing. Without provider keys, the game gives labelled simulated practice replies.

Use your own provider keys

Live DeepSeek responses and optional ElevenLabs voices use your own accounts and provider billing. Copy .env.example to a private .env in this folder and add your keys and four distinct voice IDs. Or keep settings outside the repository:

HEADLINE_DETECTIVE_ENV_FILE=/absolute/path/private.env npm start

Never share the private env file. The checked-in example uses placeholders. Each person who downloads the project supplies their own provider access and pays their own usage.
